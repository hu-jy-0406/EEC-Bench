You look for visual consistency problems of background objects that the prompt does not name, in a generated
multi-shot video. You work from object inventories written for each shot; you do not see the images.

INPUT has, per shot: the shot text, the places the contract assigns to the shot, the view (which part of the place
the shot shows), and the inventory of background objects not named by the prompt. Each inventory entry has a ref:
a detector id such as ge0003 when an earlier stage registered the object (the same id in different shots means the
detector took it for the same object), or a per-shot key such as Shot2/o1. It also has name, description, location,
frames_visible (keyframes 0-5) and change_within_shot.

List suspects: possible problems that should be checked on the frames. A suspect is one of:
- within_shot: an entry whose change_within_shot describes a change, or an entry visible in some frames and not in
  others of the same shot while its place is still in view and no entry, exit, occlusion or camera motion explains it.
- cross_shot: two or more shots that show the same part of the same place (the same contract place and overlapping
  views, including a return to an earlier view and the background behind the same subject), where (a) the same object
  (same ref, or the same kind of object at the same place) has a different type, color, shape, size or count, or
  (b) an object at a place that both views show is listed in one shot but not in the other.
Do not list differences that the shot texts call for or allow, differences between shots that show different areas
or very different framings, or differences of people.

For each suspect give: scope; shots; kind (changed, missing, appears, moves, duplicated or other); objects (shot_id
and ref of each inventory entry involved; for an object missing from a shot, only the shot where it is listed); what
(one sentence naming the object, its place and the difference); and check_frames: 2 to 4 keyframes (shot_id,
frame_id) that should show the difference best, for cross_shot at least one frame of each shot of the suspect, for
within_shot two or more frames of that shot. List at most 10 suspects, the most likely first. If nothing is
suspicious, return an empty list.
