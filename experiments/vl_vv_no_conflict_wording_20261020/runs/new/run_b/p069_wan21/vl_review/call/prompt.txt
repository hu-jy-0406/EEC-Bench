=== Shared definitions: V-L and V-V, and the boundary between them ===
Two separate questions are asked about one generated multi-shot video. This
call answers ONLY the question of its own branch, stated in the task section.
The other branch's definition is given only so that you can recognize a
finding that belongs to it and leave that finding out. A separate call
handles the other branch; nothing is lost when you leave its findings out.

V-L (video vs language)
A visible observation contradicts an explicit requirement of the text: the
global prompt, a shot prompt, the contract (entity descriptions, entity
schedule, claims) or the required shot transitions.
- Test: you can quote the violated words literally, and the content of the
  shot visibly contradicts those words. A detail the text does not state is
  never V-L.
- Entity descriptions are episode-wide. A description of an entity in the
  contract (entity_descriptions) or in the global prompt applies in every
  shot where that entity appears (scheduled in entity_schedule or visibly
  present), including shots whose text only names the entity. A shot's own
  text overrides it only where it describes that entity differently (a
  different value for the same attribute, or a stated change such as
  changing clothes); a shorter or less specific mention is not a different
  description.
- The requirement is the wording, not how another generated shot happened to
  depict it. A shot that does not conflict with the words (any blue car
  does not conflict with "a blue car") does not violate them, even if it
  looks different from another shot; that difference is a V-V question.
- Exception: explicit continuity words ("the same sofa", "remains",
  "returns to", "back in", "still", "again", "unchanged", "keeps",
  "maintain the same man") make sameness itself the requirement. A shot
  whose entity or place visibly differs from the one established by the
  other shots violates those words (stated_continuity, or attribute when a
  described attribute is also contradicted).
- V-L subcategories (the kind of requirement violated):
    presence_count: a required entity/setting is missing; wrong number of instances; an unrequested prominent extra entity; an entity required to stay offscreen is visible; the shot is black or unreadable
    attribute: described identity descriptors or properties (age, gender, species, hair, facial hair, clothing, accessories, colors, materials, marks, legible text/numbers/logos, object state such as open/closed, on/off, full/empty), including a described attribute shown on the wrong character
    action_state: a described action, motion, pose, gesture or expression is missing, wrong, incomplete, reversed, in the wrong order, or has the wrong start/end state or direction
    interaction: the wrong actor/object, or a described holding, touching, using, giving, receiving or contact relation is absent or wrong
    spatial_orientation: described positions (left/right, foreground, between, on/in/under), distances, facing or gaze directions
    setting_environment: described location type, background elements, time of day, weather, season, lighting or mood
    camera_composition: described shot scale, camera angle, viewpoint, framing, focus or camera movement
    style_rendering: described rendering style, animation vs live action, color mode, aspect/letterbox
    stated_continuity: an explicit sameness/continuity phrase (same, remains, retains, returns to, still, again, unchanged, keeps, familiar, established) is contradicted and no more specific subcategory fits
    shot_structure: the required shot transitions or shot plan are not realized (a required cut or continue, a shot's content appearing in another shot's interval, a required new shot repeating the previous one)

V-V (video vs video)
A visible observation is incompatible with other visual evidence in the same
episode, without a visible or text-supported cause.
- Test: you can cite two witnesses in the video that cannot both be true of
  the same entity, place or moment. No quote is needed; the text only tells
  which entities or places should be the same and which changes are caused.
- within_shot: frames of ONE shot cannot all be true of one continuous take,
  or an entity is physically corrupted inside the shot.
- cross_shot: two or more shots that show the same character, object, place,
  rendering style or continuous moment are mutually incompatible, or a shot
  boundary does not realize its required transition. Sameness may come from
  the text (same name or label, entity_schedule, "same", "remains",
  "returns", "again") or from clear visual evidence that the episode intends
  the same entity or place.
- A change is normal, and not a V-V problem, when a camera movement or a new
  camera position, a described or visible action, an occlusion or exit, a
  lighting or time change, elapsed story time, or a required cut explains
  it, or when the two observations are not the same entity.
- V-V subcategories, within_shot:
    identity_drift: a person/animal morphs into a different-looking individual (face shape, apparent age, skin tone, facial features)
    appearance_drift: clothing, hair, accessories, colors or marks change with no visible action
    action_discontinuity: a character's pose, position or action progress jumps, reverses or restarts between frames without continuous motion (teleport, instant pose change, action reset)
    object_instability: an object's type, shape, size, color, count, legible text or state changes with no action
    presence_instability: an entity pops in/out without entry, exit or occlusion (including vanishing for several frames and reappearing); an instance duplicates, splits, or two entities fuse into a hybrid
    background_drift: background elements or layout change in a way camera motion does not explain
    physical_integrity: malformed, melting or fused bodies, hands or faces; extra or missing limbs; interpenetration; impossible deformation; a reflection or shadow with no source
    relation_break: a held object teleports or switches hands; contact, support or relative position changes with no action
    unintended_cut: the camera setup, composition or setting jumps discontinuously between keyframes of one shot (an unrequested cut), or for an episode-level video the shot change happens deep inside this interval
    rendering_instability: flicker, color/exposure flash (often only in the first frame), black or blank frames, or a rendering-style switch inside the shot
- V-V subcategories, cross_shot:
    character_identity: the same character's face, age, gender, skin tone or build differs
    character_appearance: the same character's hairstyle/color, facial hair, clothing (garment type, color, pattern, logo, number), accessories, makeup or injuries differ, including attributes that migrate from one character to another
    object_identity: the same prop/object/vehicle/animal differs in type, shape, color, markings, legible text or size
    setting_background: a recurring location's architecture, wall/floor colors and materials, doors/windows, furniture, vegetation, signage, set dressing or layout differ
    environment_lighting: the same continuous scene changes time of day, weather, season, lighting or color grade without a supported cause
    rendering_style: photoreal vs animation, 2D vs 3D, or another visual style differs across shots that should share one style
    instance_count: a recurring character/object is duplicated, merged with another entity, replaced by a different individual, or two characters who should be distinct look identical or swap looks
    state_action_continuity: pose, position, held objects, object states, clothing state or the progress of an ongoing action is incompatible across a boundary or a return, without enough elapsed time or a shown action
    spatial_continuity: relative positions, left/right arrangement, screen direction or layout of entities flip or rearrange without a cause, including a layout that contradicts the camera change between the shots
    transition_realization: a boundary does not realize its required transition (a required cut that does not happen, or a required continue that is broken)

Boundary: what each branch reports
| Situation | V-L branch | V-V branch |
| A described attribute is wrong in the same way in every shot | report it in every violating shot | nothing to report |
| An undescribed attribute changes between shots of one scene | nothing to report | report the change |
| A described attribute changes between shots | report only the shots that contradict the words | report the change |
| Text says "same / remains / returns" and the shots differ | report stated_continuity (or attribute) in the shots that break it | report the change |
| A shot does not conflict with the words but looks different from another shot | nothing to report | report the change if the two should match |
| A change explained by an action, camera movement, occlusion or story time | nothing, unless the end state contradicts the text | nothing to report |
| A required cut is missing or a required continue is broken | report shot_structure | report transition_realization |
| A within-shot change that the text forbids ("hold this single view") | report it | report it |
| A required entity never appears | report presence_count | nothing, unless it pops in or out |
| An unrequested entity appears | report presence_count only if it is prominent | report only if it is itself inconsistent |
A detector ledger entry, a detector miss or a ledger identity link is never
evidence by itself in either branch; inspect the frames.

=== 1. Your branch ===
Judge each candidate only as a V-L issue: does a shot visibly contradict
explicit words of the text? A candidate that only shows that two shots look
different, while neither shot conflicts with the words, is not a V-L issue
(rejected, basis visual_difference_only). The candidate's wording,
subcategory and confidence are hypotheses, not evidence.

=== 2. First make each candidate clear, then decide ===
For every candidate choose an action:
- keep: the candidate is one problem. Return one unit with unit_id equal to
  the issue_id.
- split: the candidate bundles two or more independent problems that can be
  true or false separately (different requirements, different entities or
  attributes, or different shots that fail for different reasons). Return
  one unit per problem, with unit_ids "<issue_id>.1", "<issue_id>.2", ...
  and explain in split_reason. Split only real bundles; never split one
  failure into fragments, and never add a problem the candidate does not
  describe.
For every unit write description: a clear, complete Chinese statement of
the problem as you will judge it: the shot(s), the quoted requirement, what
the frames show and the mismatch. If the candidate's description was
unclear or partly inaccurate (wrong frame, wrong shot among several, wrong
color name, vague wording), correct it here, set revision to "clarified" and
say in revision_note what you changed; otherwise set revision to
"unchanged" and revision_note to "". Clarify; do not weaken: keep the full
core problem that the evidence shows. Then judge the unit as described.
A unit's shot_ids lie within the candidate's occurrence shots.

=== 3. Verdicts and their criteria ===
Judge the CORE PROBLEM of each unit: the requirement it cites is violated in
the shots it names. Inaccurate secondary details are corrected in the
description, never a reason to reject.
- confirmed (basis requirement_violated) requires ALL of:
  1. requirement_quote is a literal quote of the source text (it is checked
     by the runner);
  2. the requirement applies to the unit's shots (requirement_applies says
     why: the shot's own text, the global prompt, or an episode-wide entity
     description of an entity present in the shot, without a different
     description in the shot's own text);
  3. at least one evidence frame from the unit's shots, role "supports",
     shows content that contradicts the quoted words.
- rejected, with one basis:
  requirement_absent: the text does not state what the unit claims (say
  what the text does say);
  requirement_not_applicable: the requirement exists but does not apply to
  these shots (a different description in the shot's own text, or the
  entity is not in the shot);
  requirement_satisfied: the frames satisfy the quoted words; cite at least
  one evidence frame with role "refutes";
  visual_difference_only: the shot does not conflict with the words and only differs
  from another shot; cite at least one evidence frame.
  Never reject because the violating shot's own text only names the entity
  without restating its description, or because another shot satisfies it.
- uncertain (basis not_verifiable): the frames cannot decide (the entity is
  occluded, too small, out of frame or ambiguous); name the missing
  evidence in assessment.
Evidence roles are relative to the core problem: supports (the frame shows
the violation), refutes (the frame shows compliance), context (anything
else, including a frame that only shows that a secondary detail was wrong).
Inspect the frames yourself; use the full relevant shot, and other shots
only as context. Describe only what is visible.
