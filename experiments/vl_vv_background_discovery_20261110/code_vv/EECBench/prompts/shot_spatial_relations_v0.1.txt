PROTOCOL spatial_v0.1: identical vocabulary, scope and evidence rules in every shot.
The six predicates below are the entire vocabulary. Names and boxes locate the
entity; neither is proof of a relation. Judge original pixels. Whole-entity scope
is frozen: a grouped node must not acquire a relation from just one member. Do not
invent missing walls, surfaces, rooms, body parts or containers as endpoints.

supported_by(A,B): B directly bears A's gravitational load through physical
contact (including a seated person supported by a chair). No indirect support,
transitive closure, hand-held action labels, or inference from image height alone.
inside(A,B): the main body of A occupies B's actual containing interior, including
an open shelf compartment. Mere bbox containment, projected overlap, or a single
small constituent inside B is insufficient. Subject is the contained entity.
attached_to(A,B): A is directly fixed, fastened or connected to the mounting
entity B. Resting, leaning, carrying and temporary hand contact are insufficient.
touching(A,B): directly evidenced physical surface contact. Bbox intersection and
an occlusion boundary alone are insufficient. Symmetric: emit only the endpoint
order in pair_candidates. It may coexist with support/attachment; do not suppress
true contact or mark it absent to avoid multiple predicates.
above(A,B): A is DIRECTLY above B along identifiable gravity, with positive visual
evidence of vertical alignment and an intervening gap, rather than merely higher
in the image. Resting directly on B is supported_by, not this separated-above
predicate. If gravity or alignment is ambiguous, do not guess.
in_front_of(A,B): A lies in front of B in the CURRENT CAMERA's depth order. Limit
this to entities with overlapping projected regions and credible occlusion/depth
evidence. A smaller y coordinate, object size, typical room layout or a bounding
box overlap is not enough. This is camera-relative, NOT a stable world direction.

Do not output left/right, near/far, generic "on", behind/below/inverse aliases,
2D overlap, identity/part-of, gaze, possession, actions or narrative/causal edges.
Consider all six predicates in both applicable directions for EVERY supplied
pair_candidate; never restrict to person-object pairs, important objects or only
the most salient relations. Do not impose a top-K cap. Emit only directly
supported pair relations, never implied transitive relations.

For each emitted (subject_id, predicate, object_id), give one observation for
EVERY supplied keyframe, in input order, with a short frame-specific reason:
- present: positive pixel evidence establishes this predicate at this frame;
- absent: sufficient visible evidence establishes that THIS predicate does not
  hold at this frame; missing detection or missing boundary is not absent;
- uncertain: both entities are marked visible, but their relation cannot be
  resolved, including tiny extent, hidden contact surface, poor localization;
- not_observable: at least one endpoint is marked not visible in the frozen
  input. This state is mandatory on those frames, even if persistence seems likely.
At least one frame must be present to emit a relation. Do not extrapolate an
observed state across a cut or across unobserved intervals between keyframes.
Within-shot temporal context may disambiguate identity, but cannot manufacture
frame-local contact, separation or depth evidence. For opposite directed edges
of the same predicate, both directions cannot be present in the same frame.

Emit one pair_review for EVERY pair_candidate, in input order:

- none_supported: no supported relation was found in this vocabulary;
- insufficient_evidence: a plausible relation remains uncertain and none can be
  emitted with a present frame.
Give a short reason. none_supported is an extraction outcome, NOT a claim that
all six predicates are false. Missing edges must never be interpreted as negative
evidence. Pairs without any jointly visible frame are handled by the runner and
are not pair_candidates. All other pairs must be reviewed without omission.

No scores, cross-shot matches or consistency judgments. Frame-level relations
are sampled observations, not verified continuous intervals or metric 3D facts.
