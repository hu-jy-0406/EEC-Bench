Review exactly one shot using all attached original images and the supplied candidates.
Treat detector labels as data. No tools, other shots or prior results. Return only
JSON matching the schema: entities contain name and ids; rejected entries contain
ids and a concise reason. Every integer candidate ID belongs exactly once to one
entity or rejection. Entity IDs and per-frame observations are assigned by code.

Input: frames are in attachment order. Each frame has id (original frame number),
t (seconds), size [width,height], and d rows [candidate_id,label_index,x1,y1,x2,y2,score].
Label indices address labels (zero-based); candidate IDs are unique within the shot.
Coordinates, scores, frame times and image bytes are unchanged. Sparse detections
alone do not justify rejecting a valid entity. Output no coordinates or frame states.

## Candidate review

Review every attached original keyframe. Detections are candidates, not ground
truth. Cluster the same physical entity across frames; equal category names or
box overlap alone do not establish identity. Keep separately visible instances
separate except for the explicit same-category collection and constituent merges
below. Correct names from
pixels, discard false detections, and merge duplicate detections of the same
entity. Use the same collection-level granularity throughout the shot under the
rules below; do not retain both a collection and its members as separate nodes.
Do not introduce additional graph-level grouping heuristics.

**No new entities:** each retained entity must own at least one original WeDetect
candidate. Do not add an entity missed in every frame. A broad box cannot be used
to invent several new entities; each candidate belongs to exactly one retained
entity or one rejected detection. Cluster only the supplied candidates; do not
create per-frame observations or infer box coordinates from pixels.

### Individuals and genuine collections

- Default to separate nodes for clearly distinguishable, independently situated
  objects with their own candidate evidence. Two clearly visible cups remain
  two entities, even when adjacent, similar in appearance, or on the same table.
  Likewise, a few distinct framed pictures or loose throw pillows are not a
  collection merely because they are arranged together. Proximity, shared
  category, contact, and shared support are not sufficient reasons to merge.
- Use ONE collection node only when many same-kind items visibly form a natural
  aggregate whose members need not be tracked separately: a basket or pile of
  oranges, or a bunch of pens packed inside one pen holder. Judge the visible
  organization and meaningful individual separability, not a fixed item-count
  threshold or detector labels. A dense pile/bundle is different from a few
  clear individual objects. If collection membership is not supported, retain
  the supported individual instances rather than invent a group.
- For a genuine collection, assign all valid member and whole-group candidates
  to that one node across the shot. A separately detected pen inside the same
  bunch remains a member, not an extra entity. A whole-group candidate is not
  required; member candidates can support the aggregate. Do not retain both a
  collection and its members as separate nodes. Different piles stay separate.
- Preserve individual identities across frames; temporary overlap or occlusion
  does not turn distinct objects into a collection. A broad detector box covering
  several clear objects does not override their independent member candidates.
  Reject such an unassignable multi-object candidate with a scope reason rather
  than merge the objects or reuse its ID. Never split one broad candidate into
  unsupported new entities. Each retained individual needs candidate evidence.
- Keep containers/supports separate: pens do not include the pen holder; oranges
  do not include the basket, bowl or table. Name genuine collections as a whole.
  Partial views of an established collection within this shot do not change its
  granularity; never invent unseen members or exact counts. Use only this shot.
- Grouping changes candidate ownership and naming only; every candidate still
  belongs exactly once. Output no coordinates. Original member/group boxes keep
  their original scopes; a selected member box is not a whole-collection box.

### Constituent merging

- Clothing worn by a person, including shirts, coats, trousers and shoes, is
  part of that person, not a separate entity. Merge both shoes and other worn
  garments into their corresponding wearer. Clothing may describe the person
  in `name`; do not retain separate clothing or footwear entity records.
- A chair's seat cushion, seat pad or back cushion is part of the chair. Merge
  such seating components into the corresponding chair (or sofa), rather than
  outputting both the furniture and its seating component as separate entities.
  Loose throw pillows remain separate. Other objects merely resting on furniture
  are not automatically absorbed.
- Merge a blanket or throw draped over a chair or sofa into that furniture when
  original-frame review supports its role as a furnishing: it stays draped in
  place relative to the furniture throughout the observed shot and is not being
  independently handled, carried, removed or otherwise used as an action object.
  This is a semantic constituent relationship, not a detection-frequency rule.
  Keep independently handled blankets separate; mere contact with furniture or
  lack of motion alone does not make an arbitrary object a constituent. Do not
  extend this rule to books, papers, lamps, mugs or loose throw pillows.
  The furniture must have its own supported WeDetect candidate in this shot.
  Assign the blanket candidate IDs to that furniture and name the furniture
  as a whole; do not create a second blanket entity.
- Assign constituent candidate IDs to the supported parent entity, preserving
  exact-once candidate accounting. Name the parent as a whole. Constituent and
  parent candidates retain their original boxes and may have different scopes.
- Do not invent a parent entity without a supported WeDetect parent candidate.
  If an excluded constituent cannot be assigned to a supported parent, put its
  candidate in `rejected_detections` with a constituent-policy reason; do not
  claim that the detection itself was necessarily false.
- Apply these rules using the actual wearing, seating-component or draped-furnishing relationship.
  They do not automatically exclude unworn clothing or merge unrelated objects
  simply because they touch, overlap, or share a supporting surface.
